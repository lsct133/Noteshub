The angles

  The Angles were one of the three main Germanic tribes that migrated to Britain
during the early medieval period, alongside the Saxons and the Jutes. Here are
some key points about the Angles:

 1. 
    Origin: The Angles are believed to have originated from the region of
    Angeln, located in what is now southern Denmark and northern Germany. Their
    name is thought to derive from this area.
    
 2. 
    Migration to Britain: The Angles began migrating to Britain in the 5th
    century AD, during the period of the Anglo-Saxon settlement. They settled in
    various parts of England, particularly in the central and northern regions.
    The name &quot;England&quot; itself is derived from &quot;Angle-land,&quot;
    reflecting their significant presence in the area.
    
 3. 
    Historical Accounts: Much of what is known about the Angles comes from
    historical sources, particularly the writings of the Venerable Bede, an
    8th-century monk and historian. In his work &quot;Ecclesiastical History of
    the English People,&quot; Bede describes the arrival of the Angles and their
    settlements.
    
 4. 
    Culture and Society: The Angles, like other Germanic tribes, had a
    warrior-based society and practiced agriculture. They brought their own
    customs, language, and social structures to Britain, which contributed to
    the development of early English culture. The Old English language, which
    evolved from the dialects spoken by the Angles, Saxons, and Jutes, is the
    precursor to modern English.
    
 5. 
    Legacy: The Angles played a crucial role in the formation of early medieval
    England. Their influence can be seen in place names, language, and cultural
    practices throughout the regions they settled. The establishment of several
    kingdoms, such as Mercia and Northumbria, was largely due to the migration
    and settlement of the Angles.
    


Overall, the Angles were a significant part of the early history of England,
contributing to the cultural and social landscape of the time.
