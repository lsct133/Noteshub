Tonight in York, those two "stars" you’re seeing are actually the solar system’s heavyweights: **Venus** and **Jupiter**.

Because it is late April 2026, these two planets are currently dominating the evening sky, and they happen to be much brighter than any actual stars in those directions.

### 1. The North West "Star": **Venus**

- **Object:** The planet Venus (the "Evening Star").
    
- **Appearance:** This will be the brightest object in the sky (other than the Moon). It shines with a steady, brilliant white light and doesn't twinkle as much as a star would.
    
- **Position:** It is very low on the **North West** horizon. At 10:00 PM, it is just about to set, so if you have buildings or trees in the way, it might disappear from view shortly after.
    
### 2. The West "Star": **Jupiter**

- **Object:** The planet Jupiter.
    
- **Appearance:** While slightly less bright than Venus, Jupiter is still incredibly prominent. It has a creamier, yellowish-white glow.
    
- **Position:** Look higher up and more towards the **West**. It will be situated within the constellation **Gemini**. Since it is higher in the sky than Venus, it will stay visible for several hours longer before setting.
---
### How to Tell the Difference

A quick trick to confirm what you're looking at:

- **The Twinkle Test:** Stars twinkle because they are distant points of light easily distorted by our atmosphere. Planets are actual "discs" of light to our eyes, so their light remains much more stable and "solid."
    
- **Brightness:** Venus is currently shining at a magnitude of approximately $-3.9$ (the lower the number, the brighter the object), making it nearly 10 times brighter than the brightest actual star in the northern sky, Sirius.
### Other Stars in the Vicinity

If you see smaller, fainter stars near Jupiter, those are the "twins" of Gemini: **Castor** and **Pollux**. They will be sitting just above Jupiter tonight.

That "V" shape you’re seeing is one of the most famous shapes in the night sky! Since you are looking West, you have caught the **Hyades cluster**, which forms the face of **Taurus the Bull**.

Even though Jupiter is currently moving through this region, the V-shape itself is made of several stars.
### The Anatomy of the "V"

The Hyades is a "star cluster"—a group of hundreds of stars born at the same time—but the V-shape is dominated by a few key players:

- **Aldebaran:** This is the brightest star in the V, sitting at the "top left" (or the "eye" of the bull). It has a distinct **orange-red** tint.
    
- **The Hyades Stars:** The rest of the V is made up of several stars (like _Gamma, Delta, and Epsilon Tauri_). These are much fainter than Jupiter but clear enough to form that geometric shape.
    
- **The Alignment:** Right now, **Jupiter** is positioned very close to this cluster, making it look like a brilliant "super-star" joined the V.
---
### A Little Higher Up: The Pleiades

If you look slightly above and to the right of that V-shape, you might see a tiny, tight "shimmering" cloud of stars that looks like a miniature version of the Big Dipper. That is the **Pleiades** (the Seven Sisters).
### Why it looks like a V

In Greek mythology, the Hyades were the daughters of Atlas and the half-sisters of the Pleiades. Astronomically, they are about **150 light-years** away from us. Fun fact: **Aldebaran** (the bright red eye) isn't actually part of the cluster; it just happens to be in the same line of sight, sitting much closer to Earth at about **65 light-years** away.

Are you using binoculars, or just your naked eye? If you have binoculars handy, that V-shape will explode into dozens of tiny sparkling diamonds!
