
## Simple Guide to Hyphens & Dashes

Here's a **kid-friendly way** to understand three different punctuation marks that might look similar but do different jobs!

---
### The Three Types

| Mark | Name | What It Does | Example |
|------|------|-------------|---------|
| — | Em dash | Creates a dramatic pause or break in a sentence | She finally answered—after three days—without explanation. |
| – | En dash | Shows a range or connection between things | Pages 12–27 or the New York–London flight |
| - | Hyphen | Joins two words together to make one idea | a well-known author or twenty-one students |

---

### Em Dash (—): The Drama Dash

**Use it when:** You want to make a sentence more exciting or show an interruption.

Think of it like a big pause for effect!

- **For emphasis:** There are three choices—accept, decline, or negotiate.
- **For interruptions:** "I was going to—" He stopped, unable to finish.
- **For dramatic pauses:** She finally answered—after three days—without explanation.

---

### En Dash (–): The Range Dash

**Use it when:** You're showing a range of numbers or connecting two related places or ideas.

It means "from...to" or "between."

- **For page numbers:** Pages 12–27
- **For flight routes:** The New York–London flight
- **For time periods:** The 2005–2008 study

---

### Hyphen (-): The Connector

**Use it when:** You're joining two words together so they work as a team.

- **Before nouns:** a well-known author, a part-time job
- **In numbers:** twenty-one students, one-third of the pizza
- **With prefixes:** pre-existing, re-read, anti-bullying

---

### Quick Checklist

🎬 **Need a dramatic pause?** → Em dash (—)  
📊 **Showing a range?** → En dash (–)  
🔗 **Joining words?** → Hyphen (-)

---

## Fun Fact

These are **punctuation marks**, which means they're symbols that help organise writing. They work with grammar (how sentences are built), but they're not grammar rules themselves!
